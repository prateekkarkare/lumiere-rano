Splitting the enhancing mask into lesions: 26-connectivity, floor 20 mm³

The enhancing compartment answers "is this voxel tumour?" but not "how many lesions are there." Imposing lesion identity needs two choices — a connectivity rule and a volume floor. Both are settled here against all 93 practice-arm timepoints, not against intuition.
Decision

26-connectivity (face, edge or corner contact joins two voxels), followed by a 20 mm³ volume floor applied to the lesion inventory only.

Why 26 and not 6

The a-priori argument is that enhancing tumour presents as thin rims, one or two voxels thick after resampling, and 6-connectivity shatters a continuous rim into fragments. The sweep found a sharper argument that does not depend on that intuition.

At a 20 mm³ floor there are 2 timepoints where 6-connectivity ends up with FEWER surviving lesions than 26, not more. It cannot merge anything — connectivity only ever splits — so the only way this happens is that 6-connectivity fragments a real lesion into pieces that individually fall below the floor and are then discarded.

The failure this avoids. 6-connectivity combined with a floor does not merely over-count debris; it can delete genuine lesions outright. That is the over-merge failure mode — a lesion that silently stops existing — arriving through a side door. 26-connectivity cannot produce it, because any component it forms is a superset of the 6-connected one.

Choosing the floor

The floor is a hygiene filter on the lesion inventory: at 1 mm atlas resolution, resampled from acquisitions with ~5 mm slices, isolated few-voxel components are partial-volume and interpolation debris rather than tumour. Its cost is not volume — even at 100 mm³ the floor discards well under 1% of enhancing volume. Its cost is erasure.

20 mm³ sits where the two curves cross usefully. It is anchored, not tuned:

  • Far below measurability. A lesion satisfying RANO's 10 × 10 mm bar on at least two slices occupies on the order of 100–200 mm³. A 20 mm³ floor is 5–10× below anything that could ever be a target lesion, so it cannot remove one.
  • At the scale of the acquisition. A single native voxel on a 0.9 × 0.9 × 5 mm grid is about 4 mm³; a 3 × 3 in-plane footprint on one native slice is roughly 36 mm³. Components below 20 mm³ are smaller than anything independently observable in the data the segmentation was computed from.
  • Still sensitive to new lesions. The floor must stay well under measurability precisely because a new, non-measurable enhancing focus is still evidence of progression. 20 mm³ keeps those.
The constraint this creates. At 20 mm³, 7 timepoints lose their entire lesion inventory (enhancing volumes 3, 3, 17, 21, 27, 28, 48 mm³). Those timepoints still have enhancing disease. An empty inventory therefore means "no measurable target lesion" and must never be read as "no disease" or allowed to produce a CR. Volumetry never applies the floor — it sums every voxel — and the response call reads volume, not inventory size. This is the same distinction measurement.py already enforces between None and False.

What the floor actually removes

no floor floor 20 mm³
Lesion counts per timepoint, before and after. Unfloored, the median timepoint carries 4 components and the worst carries 33. After the floor the median is 1. Genuine multifocality survives: 18 timepoints still carry three or more lesions.

Two constraints this puts on the measurement step

One target lesion is not enough

Share of enhancing volume held by the largest lesion, one point per timepoint, sorted. Median 97.2%, but the minimum is 29.0%.

In 28 of 83 timepoints the largest lesion holds under 90% of the enhancing volume. Selecting a single biggest target and measuring only that would discard the majority of the disease in the worst cases. RANO's sum of products across several target lesions is not a formality — this arm needs it.

Cross-sections are routinely in several pieces

Across every axial slice occupied by a largest lesion (2,045 slices in total), 50.4% show that single lesion as more than one disjoint 2-D piece — the lesion is connected through adjacent slices, not within the slice. Only 1 timepoint is fragmented on every one of its slices, and 18 are never fragmented. Any per-slice diameter measurement must handle a multi-piece cross-section as the normal case, and must never draw a caliper across the gap between two pieces.
