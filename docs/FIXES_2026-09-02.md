# Three fixes from the 2026-08-31 adjudication, and what they were worth

*Written 2026-09-02. Companion to `ADJUDICATION_2026-08-31.md`, which is the diagnosis; this is
the treatment and the honest bill.*

| # | Fix | Where | Adjudication ref |

## Fix 3 in detail — the only one that is new code

The old rule searched for the first scan **at least** `confirmation_weeks` out and treated it as
the confirmation, however far away it was. Adjudication case 4 is the demonstration: Patient-009's
week-024 complete response was demoted to SD by a scan **42 weeks later** that showed the tumour
back. That scan is not failing to confirm a response; it is a different phase of the disease.

The fix makes confirmation a **three-way** outcome instead of a two-way one:

| Outcome | Condition | Effect on the call |
|---|---|---|
| confirmed | eligible scan within `[confirmation_weeks, confirmation_max_weeks]`, scoring a response | kept |
| refuted | same window, scoring something else | demoted to SD, `provisional_call` records what it was |
| **unconfirmable** | nearest eligible scan is beyond `confirmation_max_weeks`, **or** there is no later scan at all | **kept**, flagged in `unknowns` |

The two unconfirmable routes are flagged separately — `confirmation_too_late` (a hole in the
follow-up) and `confirmation_followup` (a follow-up that ended) — because they are different
facts about the record.

The limit is applied to the **earliest** eligible scan, never searched past. Skipping a too-late
scan to reach a friendlier one further out would reintroduce exactly the rebound-confirmation bug
that "first eligible scan, not best" exists to prevent.

`confirmation_max_weeks = None` restores the old unlimited behaviour.

The flag fires on **22 timepoints** across the 91-patient set.

## Choosing the two numbers — `scripts/sweep_guards.py`

Both constants were guesses, so they were swept. **The held-out arm was excluded** (17 patients,
155 timepoints): choosing a constant by reading a curve is selection on data, and that arm is
spent once, in September, on the finished rule. The sweep therefore runs on 74 patients / 221
timepoints.

### `min_absolute_change_mm3` — there is no best value, and that is the finding

```
0 - 40 mm3      identical output, byte for byte
45 - 150 mm3    agreement flat, balanced accuracy +4 pp
>= 200 mm3      four correct PD calls destroyed
```

Two things to take from this, both uncomfortable:

**The bug this was meant to fix has zero incidence.** Not one scored call in the cohort turns on
an absolute change below 40 mm³. Setting the floor to 1, 3 or 10 mm³ produces *the same output as
leaving it off*. The failure mode — one voxel scored as progression — is real in principle and
does not occur here.

**The +4 pp at 45 mm³ is one patient.** It is two adjacent timepoints in Patient-059, and it is
bought by turning a correct PD in Patient-062 into a wrong SD. PR support in this cohort is 12
scans, so two of them are 17 points of PR recall. That is not a calibration; it is a sample of
two, and taking it would be fitting.

**Shipped: 10.0 mm³** — the largest value that provably changes nothing. Native voxels on this
cohort run 1–5 mm³ (the 1 mm atlas grid is resampled, not acquired), so 10 mm³ is roughly 2–10
acquired voxels. It states the intent without buying agreement it cannot defend.

And it is **not** the measurability gate. Finding F3 settled that: measurability is a shape test
(≥10 mm in two perpendicular axes) and no volume threshold can express it — a 30×30×3 mm pancake
is 1,414 mm³ and not measurable; a 12×11×10 mm blob is 691 mm³ and is.

Adjudication case 7 (Patient-032 week-085, 3 mm³ → 28 mm³ scored as "+833%") is **not fixed** by
any value on the curve. The adjudication predicted this: it needs the nadir reset across surgery,
which is fix 3 on *that* document's list and is still open.

### `confirmation_max_weeks` — a real plateau, on a small margin

```
<= 12 wk        SD recall falls; the limit starts rejecting genuine refutations
16 - 18 wk      flat optimum
19 - 24 wk      one correct CR lost
>= 27 wk        decays back to the unlimited behaviour
```

**Shipped: 16.0 weeks.** Follow-up on this cohort runs every 8–12 weeks, so 16 admits the next
scheduled scan plus a slipped one and rejects a scan a whole extra interval away. The scheduling
argument and the swept optimum agree, which is the only reason to trust either: the measured
margin over unlimited is **+1.2 pp balanced accuracy off a net of one timepoint**, which alone
would be noise.

On the full 376 the same fix is worth +4.1 pp balanced accuracy (CR recall 42→50%, PR 10→20%),
because the held-out arm was selected for having complete responses and is response-rich. Do not
read that as validation — read it as a reminder that the excluded arm is not a random sample.

**Take fix 3 for correctness, not for the number.** Demoting a response because no scan exists to
confirm it reads "we have no evidence" as "the response failed". Those are different, and only one
of them is in the data.

## Caveats that carry over unchanged

Everything in `ADJUDICATION_2026-08-31.md` §8 still applies. The two that bind hardest here:

* **A1** — `RT_END_WEEK = 10.0` is one cohort-wide constant, not a per-patient fact. The dataset
  ships no radiotherapy dates. **Fix 1, the largest of the three, rests entirely on it.**
* **A3** — volume uncertainty is size-dependent (±36.6 pp under 1,000 mm³) and the rule engine
  still does not read `VOLUME_UNCERTAINTY_PP`. A size-dependent band, not a constant floor, is the
  right instrument for fix 2 and remains unbuilt.
