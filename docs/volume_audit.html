Atlas-space volumetry is unbiased — but its precision depends on lesion size, not on the compartment

Every DeepBraTumIA compartment volume computed five ways — once on the MNI 1 mm atlas grid, and once on each of the four native acquisition grids — across 90 patients and 591 timepoints.
Read this first — a label-decode error was found and corrected during this audit.

DeepBraTumIA's label 1 is contrast-enhancing and label 2 is necrosis / non-enhancing. label_schema.py had these two swapped, inherited from the Label name column of LUMIERE-pyradiomics-deepbratumia-features.csv, which is itself mislabeled. Three independent checks agree: the shipped measured_volumes_in_mm3.json matches label 1 to Enhancing_Core in 599/599 masks; contrast physics (post- minus pre-contrast T1, normalised per scan) shows label 1 enhancing in 51/52 sampled cases while label 2 loses signal (median −0.073); and the resulting trajectories are clinically coherent. HD-GLIO-AUTO was re-tested the same way and is correct as documented. Any earlier analysis that treated label 2 as enhancing was measuring necrosis and must be re-run. Two things are visible at once. The median sits on zero at every anisotropy — nearest-neighbour resampling is unbiased, it does not systematically inflate or shrink a compartment. But the spread fans out sharply with slice thickness. It appears to fan out fastest for contrast-enhancing tissue; the next section shows that appearance is a confound.

3 · It is not the compartment — it is the size

The obvious reading of chart 2 is "the enhancing compartment is the fragile one". That reading is a confound. Resampling error is created at a structure's boundary, so what matters is surface area relative to volume — and small structures have a worse ratio than large ones, whatever tissue they are. Enhancing compartments simply tend to be the smallest of the three. Control for size and the compartments converge.

The spread is a function of how big the thing you are measuring is, and it spans nearly three orders of magnitude. On an isotropic grid it never exceeds 2 percentage points at any size. On a thick-slice grid a 60 cm³ mass still measures to within about 1.5 points — but a sub-1 cm³ compartment swings by 37 points, which is larger than a RANO response threshold.

4 · What this settles

Atlas-space volumetry is safe to build on, and needs no bias correction. All 2,396 .tfm transforms are rigid (determinant 1 to 1e-10), and the measurements confirm the consequence: median deviation is 0.00% at every anisotropy and every size. Nearest-neighbour resampling adds noise, not bias. Piece 2 and the RANO engine can work in atlas space without a Jacobian correction.

The error bar must be a function of lesion size, not a single constant. Quoting one tolerance for "a volume" would be wrong by a factor of 20 at the extremes. For a large tumour on any grid, and for anything at all on an isotropic grid, the tolerance is well under 2%. For a sub-1 cm³ compartment on 6–7 mm slices it is ±18%.

That worst case is clinically live, and it is the case RANO cares most about. A 37-point spread is the same size as a RANO response threshold, so on small lesions the choice of grid alone could flip a call. Small enhancing foci are exactly what early-progression and new-lesion detection deal with — so Piece 4 must carry a size-dependent confidence, and any new-lesion rule needs a minimum-volume floor beneath which a "change" is not reported as real.

Field-of-view clipping is not a risk here. Zero masks in the cohort touch the atlas boundary, so the 182×218×182 mm box is large enough for every tumour in LUMIERE. Keep it as a check — a future case can violate it — but it needs no mitigation today.

Caveat that limits the whole claim. The four native masks are not four independent measurements of the tumour — they are one segmentation, performed once in atlas space, resampled four ways. Everything above measures resampling uncertainty. Segmentation uncertainty is larger and remains unmeasured, because LUMIERE ships no manual masks.
